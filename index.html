About
I'm an interdisciplinary designer, researcher, and design educator based in Providence, Rhode Island.
I work with individuals and academic, cultural, and community institutions, and have offered pro bono design services to volunteer-run, grassroots organizations since 2016. Across self-driven and comissioned projects, I am interested in using graphic design as a tool for critical inquiry into, and collective sense-making of, the relationship between lived experience and the material conditions that shape it—engaging visual, written, spoken, and material language.
I am currently working on freelance web and publication design projects, designing for Institute for Contemporary Art/Boston, teaching graphic design, and developing a publication titled countersense. Email is open for inquiries into projects, teaching, workshops, or to request my full CV.

Girlhood Arrested: How Girlhood is Shaped by the Carceral Landscape in Louisiana
2025
Designer, Co-author, and Editor
with The Visiting Room Project and Dr. Kennedi Johnson

What began as a zine and self-publishing workshop with The Visiting Room Project (TVRP) developed into a 230-page book initiated by my collaborator Kennedi Johnson and me. Girlhood Arrested: How Girlhood is Shaped by the Carceral Landscape in Louisiana is a reflection on the Girlhood Zine Workshop we held, the possibilities of girlhood as an organizing construct of self outside of incarceration, and is a transhistorical engagement with the archival gaps of women’s lives and stories. It contains zine facsimiles and t-shirts by participants, essays, interviews, and archival material from the Louisiana state archives.
The design takes inspiration from disciplinary forms in prison. Throughout the book, text and images run over and outside of the gridded lines with no regard for attempts to fix or constrain—a conceptual move reflecting the formation of identity outside of the context of incarceration
